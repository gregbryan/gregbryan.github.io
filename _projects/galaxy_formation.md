Galaxies grow through a competition between gas accretion, star formation, and feedback, and numerical simulations are our best tool for following that interplay across cosmic time. My group develops and runs cosmological and idealized simulations that follow dark matter, gas, stars, and black holes, with particular attention to how feedback regulates star formation {% cite 2025ApJ...994..174I %}.

A major theme of recent work is making the "subgrid" physics of large-volume simulations more predictive by building it on resolved, small-scale calculations: implementing pressure-regulated, feedback-modulated star formation models derived from parsec-scale ISM simulations {% cite 2024ApJ...975..151H 2025MNRAS.544.1390B %}, and developing the Arkenstone model for capturing fast, hot galactic winds that coarse simulations cannot resolve {% cite 2024MNRAS.527.1216S 2024MNRAS.535.3550S 2026arXiv260503154S %}.

We also work closely with observations, for example inferring the intrinsic 3D shapes of high-redshift galaxies seen by JWST {% cite 2024ApJ...963...54P %} and modeling how dust reshapes the ultraviolet and infrared emission of the earliest galaxies {% cite 2026ApJ..1003..170S %}.
